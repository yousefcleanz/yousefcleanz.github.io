What paint correction fixes

Those spiderweb swirls you see in direct sunlight usually come from automatic car washes, dirty towels, and dry wiping. They sit in the clear coat and scatter light, which makes paint look dull and hazy even right after a wash.

Paint correction levels the clear coat with a machine polisher so those marks disappear instead of just being filled in for a few weeks. The result is clear, glossy paint that reflects light the way it did when the car was new.

One-step or two-step?

One-step is a single polish that removes most light swirls and boosts gloss. It's a great fit for daily drivers in decent shape.

Two-step starts with a heavier cut to tackle deeper defects, then follows with a finishing polish that refines the paint to a mirror finish. It needs a garage or covered space.

Want the results to last? Follow correction with a ceramic coating to lock in the shine for years.

FAQ

Paint correction questions

Will paint correction remove every scratch?

It removes swirl marks and light scratches that sit in the clear coat. A scratch deep enough to catch your fingernail, or one that goes through to the primer, can't be polished out safely. We'll tell you up front what to expect.

Which do I need, one-step or two-step?

One-step is great for paint with light swirls. Two-step is for deeper defects or when you want a true mirror finish. Send a quote request and we'll help you pick.

Do you need a garage?

One-step can be done in your driveway. Two-step needs a garage or covered space.

How much does paint correction cost?

It depends on your vehicle's size, the paint's condition, and whether you choose one-step or two-step. Quotes are free, and we'll get back to you the same day.

Should I get a ceramic coating after?

It's the best way to protect freshly corrected paint. A ceramic coating keeps the gloss for years and makes washes easier. It's quoted separately.
